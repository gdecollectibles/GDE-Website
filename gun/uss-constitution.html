The Story of Old Ironsides

The Naval Act of 1794 ordered six frigates for a new United States Navy, and USS Constitution was one of them. She was launched in Boston on October 21, 1797, fastened with copper bolts and spikes supplied by Paul Revere, her hull of southern live oak up to 21 inches thick.

On August 19, 1812, she defeated HMS Guerriere, and as British shot bounced off her sides her sailors named her “Old Ironsides.” That December, under William Bainbridge, she defeated HMS Java off the coast of Brazil. In 1830 Oliver Wendell Holmes's poem “Old Ironsides” saved her from the scrapyard. Never defeated in battle, she is today the oldest commissioned warship afloat, still crewed by US Navy sailors.

The USS Constitution 1911 honors that ship. It is a commemorative custom Colt 1911 pistol in .45 ACP, engraved and hand plated in 24K gold, with pearl blue grips bearing the gold Colt Horse, and a limited edition of 300.
