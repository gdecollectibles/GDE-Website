The Story of El Alamo

In 1718 Spanish missionaries founded Mission San Antonio de Valero beside the San Antonio River. Around 1803 a Spanish cavalry company from Álamo de Parras was posted there, and the name “Alamo” stuck.

In December 1835 Texian forces took San Antonio after a five-day battle and made the Alamo their fort. General Santa Anna's army arrived on February 23, 1836, and after a 13-day siege the Mexican army stormed the walls before dawn on March 6. The State of Texas bought the Alamo church in 1883, and in 1905 Clara Driscoll paid to save the mission's Long Barrack, becoming known as the “Savior of the Alamo.”

El Alamo tells that history from both sides of the border. It is a custom Colt Government Model 1911 pistol in .38 Super, with gold-filled engraving over polished stainless steel, swirled brown pearl grips bearing the Estados Unidos Mexicanos medallion, and a limited edition of 300.
