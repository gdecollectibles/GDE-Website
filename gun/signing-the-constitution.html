The Story of the Constitution

On April 18, 1775, Paul Revere rode through the night to warn that British troops were marching on Lexington and Concord. Fifteen months later, on July 4, 1776, the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence in Philadelphia.

In the same city, on May 25, 1787, the Constitutional Convention opened with George Washington as its president. On September 17, thirty-nine delegates signed the Constitution in the room where independence had been declared, its final text written by Gouverneur Morris and opening with the words “We the People.” When New Hampshire ratified it on June 21, 1788, it took effect. It is the oldest written national constitution still in use, amended only 27 times.

The US Constitution 1911 honors that founding document. It is a commemorative custom Colt 1911 pistol in .45 ACP, with relief engraving hand plated in 24K gold, black grips bearing a 24K gold Presidential Seal, and a limited edition of 300.
