The Story of Mount Rushmore

In 1923 historian Doane Robinson proposed a giant carving in South Dakota's Black Hills to draw visitors to the state. On October 4, 1927, sculptor Gutzon Borglum and his crew began carving the granite of Mount Rushmore.

Over the next fourteen years about 400 workers removed some 450,000 tons of rock, most of it with dynamite. Borglum died in March 1941, and his son Lincoln finished the work that October. Washington, Jefferson, Theodore Roosevelt and Lincoln stand for the birth, growth, development and preservation of the nation, each face about 60 feet tall on a peak 5,725 feet above sea level.

The Mt. Rushmore 1911 honors that monument. It is a commemorative custom Colt 1911 pistol in .45 ACP, with relief engraving hand plated in 24K gold, 24K gold grips bearing a nickel Presidential Seal, and a limited edition of 300.
