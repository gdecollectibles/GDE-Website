The Story Behind the White House Masonic

On October 13, 1792, Freemasons laid the cornerstone of the White House in a ceremony, and the next year George Washington, himself a Freemason, laid the Capitol's cornerstone wearing a Masonic apron. John Adams became the first president to live in the White House on November 1, 1800.

British troops burned the house on August 24, 1814, and its first architect, James Hoban, rebuilt it. Theodore Roosevelt made “White House” its official name in 1901, and in 1935 the pyramid and all-seeing eye of the Great Seal first appeared on the one-dollar bill.
